[Time: 00:00]
Doctor: Hello, Marcus. I understand you served in the Marines?
Patient: Yeah. Two tours. Iraq and Afghanistan.
Doctor: Thank you for your service. What brings you to the VA clinic today?
Patient: My wife keeps nagging me to come. She says I’m... different. Angry all the time.
Doctor: Do you notice that anger?
Patient: I just have a short fuse. People are idiots. Driving on the freeway... if someone cuts me off, I go from zero to hundred instantly.

[Time: 01:00]
Doctor: Any nightmares?
Patient: Every night. Always the same one. The IED explosion. I wake up sweating, heart pounding. I usually sleep on the couch so I don’t wake my wife.
Doctor: Do you avoid certain places or things that remind you of your service?
Patient: I don’t do crowds. I can’t go to the mall or concerts. I need to see the exits. I sit with my back to the wall at restaurants.
Doctor: Are you startling easily?
Patient: (Laughs humorlessly) Yeah. If a door slams, I jump out of my skin. My kids popped a balloon at a birthday party last week and I nearly tackled someone. It’s embarrassing.

[Time: 02:30]
Doctor: Have you been drinking more to cope with this?
Patient: A six-pack a night. It’s the only way I can sleep without the dreams.
Doctor: Any intrusive thoughts or flashbacks during the day?
Patient: Sometimes. The smell of diesel fuel triggers it. Or seeing trash on the side of the road. I’m back there for a split second.

[Time: 03:30]
Doctor: Marcus, you’re describing classic symptoms of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder. Hyperarousal, avoidance, intrusion. It’s very common and treatable.
Patient: I don’t want to be drugged up.
Doctor: Medication is one tool, specifically SSRIs like Sertraline can help with the anger and hypervigilance. But therapy is the main treatment. Have you heard of Prolonged Exposure or CPT?
Patient: No.
Doctor: They are very effective therapies to help process the trauma so it doesn't control your life anymore.
